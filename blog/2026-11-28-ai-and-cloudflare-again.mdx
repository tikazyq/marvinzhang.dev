Suppose on a weekend afternoon you ask an AI assistant to build a small web app: a personal portfolio, a habit tracker, a bill splitter, or a quick dashboard for your own work. Ten minutes later, it runs cleanly on your laptop. That is when the real questions hit: **where does this thing live so other people—or your own phone—can open it? What will it cost every month? And what happens if it breaks or suddenly gets a spike of traffic?**

I hit a version of this myself this week when I wanted to open one of my local tools from outside my home network. Years ago, that kind of chore meant renting a server, wrestling with network ports, and worrying about security. This time I gave Cloudflare another try and found the process far smoother than I remembered. Digging into what Cloudflare has changed over the past two years made one thing clear: it is no longer just a networking and security tool built for operations engineers.

{/* truncate */}

In [*The Last Mile of AI Is Infrastructure, Not Intelligence*](/blog/ai-last-mile-infrastructure), I wrote that as AI makes generating code cheap, the hard part shifts to getting that code safely in front of real users. This post skips enterprise architecture jargon and asks a practical question: **is Cloudflare actually becoming easier to use—and a better choice—for solo developers and non-technical people who use AI to build things, and where does it sit next to mainstream options like Vercel, Netlify, Supabase, and AWS?**

Here is the short answer up front: **for small websites, personal tools, and early prototypes, Cloudflare is noticeably easier and cheaper to start on than it was two years ago. Still, easy to start does not mean effortless forever—domain setup, platform lock-in, and serving users in mainland China remain real hurdles.**

## From AI-generated code to a live link, the path got shorter

Let us put the only three Cloudflare names you actually need in plain English first. For more than a decade, most people knew **Cloudflare** as the invisible network layer that makes websites load faster and keeps bad traffic out. Today it also wants to be where your app actually lives:

- **Cloudflare Workers**: Cloudflare's main all-in-one place to host both your website and its lightweight backend code, without renting or maintaining a server.
- **Cloudflare Pages**: Cloudflare's older, separate tool for static websites; its [official docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/get-started/) now steer new projects straight to Workers so you only have one place to manage.
- **Cloudflare Tunnel**: A private, encrypted bridge that lets a tool running on your own computer be reached from anywhere, without exposing your home network to the public internet.

For years, people bounced off Cloudflare because getting started felt fragmented. You jumped between separate dashboards, split webpages from backend code, and struggled to see why something broke. Over the past two years, Cloudflare has smoothed out much of that friction.

First, the dashboard finally came together. On October 12, 2026, Cloudflare [merged its two separate admin panels into one unified navigation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-10-12-unified-dashboard-navigation/), so managing a site and controlling who can access it no longer means switching consoles. Troubleshooting is clearer too, moving from a [refreshed observability view](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-02-06-observability-ui-refresh/) in February 2026 to [platform-wide request tracing in public beta](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-tracing/) on October 2, so when a page fails you have a better chance of spotting where it stopped.

Second, publishing updates takes far fewer moving parts. Connect your code repository once, and [built-in builds](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/ci-cd/builds/) (launched September 25, 2025) automatically publish changes when you save them. Since September 22, 2026, [preview links](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-09-22-worker-previews/) give every draft change its own isolated test URL, so you can check the result before touching your live site.

Most tellingly, Cloudflare reshaped its front door for AI coding assistants. It placed an [AI-readable index](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/llms.txt) right at the top of its docs, launched [official tool connections](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/model-context-protocol/cloudflare/servers-for-cloudflare/) covering more than 2,500 actions so an assistant can inspect or configure a project on your behalf, and added [60-minute temporary previews that do not even require an account](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-06-19-temporary-accounts-for-agents/) on June 19, 2026. An AI can generate a page and hand you a working link to click before you even sign up. As I argued in [*Stack Selection in the Agent Era*](/blog/stack-selection-in-the-agent-era), short feedback loops decide which tools win when AI is doing the drafting. Here, Cloudflare clearly gets it.

## Pricing built for solo builders: $0 to $5 a month

When an individual builder or non-technical creator picks a hosting platform, two fears loom largest: paying monthly server rent for a side project nobody uses yet, or waking up to a surprise cloud bill because a post went viral or bots hammered the site overnight.

Cloudflare's biggest draw for individuals is a simple pricing ladder designed to remove surprise bandwidth bills. As of November 28, 2026, here is what a typical small project gets on the free tier versus the $5/month paid tier:

| What you want to run | Free tier ($0/mo) | $5/mo paid tier | Fine print to know up front |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Site + small backend** ([Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/)) | Unlimited static traffic; 100,000 dynamic requests/day per account | Includes 10 million requests/month ($0.30 per extra million), **no extra bandwidth fees** | Free tier returns errors once capped; paid accounts can set [spend limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/spend-management/) to pause new backend calls while static pages stay live |
| **Images & file storage** ([Storage pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)) | 10 GB storage, 1M writes, 10M reads/month | $0.015/GB-month beyond free tier, **zero download bandwidth fees** | Usage rounds up to the next billing unit (1,000,001 operations bills as 2 million) |
| **Lightweight database** ([Database pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/)) | 5M rows read/day, 100,000 writes/day, 5 GB total ([500 MB per database](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits/)) | Bundled in the $5 plan: 25B rows read/mo, 50M writes/mo, 5 GB | Reads count rows *scanned*, not returned; unindexed AI-written queries can burn quota fast |
| **Private local access & login gate** ([Tunnel](https://blog.cloudflare.com/tunnel-for-everyone/) · [Login protection](https://www.cloudflare.com/plans/zero-trust-services/)) | Named tunnels free; login protection free for up to 50 users | Starts at $7/user/month past 50 users or for longer log retention | Free login gate keeps only 24 hours of logs, which is fine for personal or small-group tools |

As I wrote in [*Cheap Code, Scarce Attention*](/blog/cheap-code-scarce-attention), once writing code gets cheap, your attention becomes the scarce resource. Previously, launching a small site with logins, image uploads, and saved data meant signing up for three or four separate services, attaching credit cards, and wiring environment variables together. Putting the whole set under one roof for $0 to $5 a month removes a lot of that drag.

Two details bring real peace of mind. First, **no surprise download bandwidth fees**: if an image-heavy page gets shared widely, you do not get hit with traditional cloud data-transfer charges. Second, **a real spending brake**: on the paid plan, setting a spend limit means a runaway loop or sudden traffic spike pauses new backend calls instead of running up an open-ended credit card bill.

:::note Why can Cloudflare offer such a generous free tier?
It is not charity. Cloudflare announced in [October 2026](https://www.cloudflare.com/press/press-releases/2026/more-than-10-million-developers-are-building-on-cloudflare/) that over 10 million developers now build on its platform, up from [over 4.5 million](https://www.cloudflare.net/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/CORRECTED-TRANSCRIPT-Cloudflare-Inc-NET-US-Q4-2025-Earnings-Call-10-February-2026-5-00-PM-ET.pdf) at the end of 2025. Low-friction free tiers bring individuals and small teams in the door; enterprise security and large-scale cloud customers pay the bills.

## Why buying Evan You's VoidZero matters when you build with AI

In June 2026, a major frontend acquisition made headlines: **VoidZero**, the company founded by **Evan You** (creator of Vue and Vite), officially [joined Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/voidzero-joins-cloudflare/) ([Evan You's announcement](https://voidzero.dev/posts/voidzero-cloudflare)). Neither blog post named a price, but Cloudflare's [Q2 2026 SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1477333/000147733326000054/cloud-20260630.htm) put the figures on the record: $164.2 million in stock and cash, plus $106.4 million in separate equity incentives.

If you do not follow web developer news, it is fair to ask why you should care.

The reason is **Vite**—the fast build engine that turns raw web code into a working site in milliseconds. Ask a modern AI coding assistant to build you a web app today, and there is a strong chance the project it generates runs on Vite. Until recently, moving an AI-generated Vite app onto Cloudflare meant fiddling with plugins, config files, and the occasional maddening bug where everything worked on your laptop and broke online.

Bringing Evan You's team inside Cloudflare turns that path into a first-class priority. On October 15, 2026, Cloudflare followed up with a [dedicated hosting service built specifically for Vite](https://blog.cloudflare.com/deploy-with-void/), aimed at making the jump from generated Vite code to a live site work out of the box.

Predictably, developers immediately asked whether Vite would get locked inside Cloudflare. Both Cloudflare and the [Vite core team](https://vite.dev/blog/cloudflare-supports-vite) pledged to keep Vite and its companion tools open source under the MIT license and deployable anywhere. So far, actions back up those words: [Vite+ 1.0 shipped under the MIT license](https://voidzero.dev/posts/announcing-vite-plus-1-0) on September 28, 2026, and on November 9 Cloudflare awarded the first [$301,300 of its $1 million Vite Ecosystem Fund](https://blog.cloudflare.com/launching-vite-ecosystem-fund/) across 44 open-source projects and maintainers.

Still, the skepticism voiced in the [June 2026 Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48398055) is healthy. Cloudflare did not spend over $160 million purely out of goodwill—it wants to win builders early from rivals like Vercel right when a project starts. For you as a builder, that competition pays off in smoother tooling today, as long as you remember that standard web code stays portable if you keep your setup clean.

## The honest catch: lock-in, real limits, and where beginners still get stuck

Look only at the upside and it is tempting to treat Cloudflare as an effortless default. In practice, every convenience carries a bill, and putting more of your app inside one platform comes with four clear tradeoffs.

![Cloudflare smooth path vs where beginners still hit walls: $0 to $5/mo works smoothly for personal sites, light tools, zero-egress file storage, and spend caps; domain/DNS setup, migration lock-in, heavy databases or standard Linux backend software, and mainland China access limits remain real hurdles](/img/blog/2026-11-28-ai-and-cloudflare-again/figure-2-fit-vs-stuck.en.png)

**First, easy to move in, harder to move out (lock-in).** A plain static website is trivial to move anywhere. Once your app leans on Cloudflare's built-in database, file storage hooks, and server-side bindings, moving to another host stops being a copy-paste job—you have to rewrite how your app reads and saves data. There is also a runtime catch: Workers only implements a [subset of standard Node.js features](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/nodejs/), and its [Python support](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/languages/python/) runs inside a specialized sandbox. When an AI writes backend code assuming a normal Linux server, it can fail on Workers in ways that leave a non-technical builder baffled.

**Second, real ceilings and single-vendor outage risk.** The free and $5 tiers are built for lightweight apps. On the free tier, each dynamic request gets just [10 milliseconds](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/) of active compute time, and each free database tops out at [500 MB](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits/). That is plenty for a personal tracker or small tool, but heavy databases, video processing, or traditional server software quickly outgrow it.

Putting your domain, hosting, and data on one provider also means one outage shakes everything at once. That is not hypothetical: on December 5, 2025, a test configuration change hit a legacy bug and caused [roughly 28% of Cloudflare's HTTP traffic to return errors](https://blog.cloudflare.com/5-december-2025-outage/) for 25 minutes. Even after Cloudflare completed a major [fault-isolation overhaul](https://blog.cloudflare.com/code-orange-fail-small-complete/) in May 2026, [its dashboard and API still suffered a 2-hour-14-minute outage on November 26, 2026](https://www.cloudflarestatus.com/incidents/xiqj67vl5pqm). A personal project can usually live with an occasional hiccup, but you should go in with eyes open.

**Third, three places where beginners still get stuck.**

- **Connecting your own domain**: The free default URL works right away, but attaching your own `.com` still means changing nameservers or editing DNS records. For non-technical builders, that remains the scariest step—one wrong edit can temporarily break existing email or your main website.
- **Debugging when AI code breaks**: When things go right, you are live in minutes. When a build fails or a missing environment variable breaks a page, the error logs still assume you know how to read developer stack traces.
- **A crowded dashboard off the happy path**: Merging the two consoles helped, yet Cloudflare is still a sprawling enterprise platform underneath. One stray click in the sidebar drops a beginner into dozens of menus built for corporate security teams.

**Fourth, the reality of serving users in mainland China.** If your main audience lives in mainland China, Cloudflare's standard global network brings real friction. A 2026 [research study](https://secretlab.page/publications/block-a-mole-arxiv-2026.pdf) using public measurement data found that 984 out of 1,100 tested default `workers.dev` addresses could not be reached directly from mainland China. Even if you attach your own custom domain, cross-border congestion causing slow loads and packet loss is a limitation Cloudflare itself acknowledges on [its blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/china-express/).

Cloudflare does offer a compliant mainland China option operated with JD Cloud ([Cloudflare China Network](https://developers.cloudflare.com/china-network/)), **but it is only sold to Enterprise customers**, requires government ICP website registration and content review for each domain, and [explicitly prohibits creating local file-storage buckets or binding custom domains for them inside mainland China](https://developers.cloudflare.com/china-network/reference/available-products/). For an individual building for users inside mainland China, a domestic cloud host will save you far more headache.

## How Cloudflare compares to Vercel, Netlify, Supabase, and AWS

Once you see Cloudflare's pricing and ceilings, the next question is obvious: **where does Cloudflare sit next to Vercel, Netlify, Supabase, and AWS—the mainstream names almost every builder hears about?**

Each of these five services solves a slightly different job. For a regular person putting an AI-built app online, four things matter most: what the service is actually for, what it costs now and when traffic grows, how approachable the dashboard is, and when picking it over Cloudflare makes more sense. Here is how they line up as of November 28, 2026:

| Provider | What it is built for | Free tier & first paid step | How bills grow with traction | When it beats Cloudflare |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cloudflare** ([Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/) · [Spend limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/spend-management/)) | All-in-one hosting for static sites, small backends, file storage, and light databases | $0 free; $5/mo paid tier (includes 10M requests/mo) | Zero download bandwidth fees and hard spend limits keep solo scaling very cheap | Solo tools, global lightweight apps, and image-heavy sites where surprise traffic bills worry you |
| **Vercel** ([Pricing](https://vercel.com/pricing) · [Pro plan](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/pro-plan) · [Hobby terms](https://vercel.com/legal/terms)) | Modern frontend and full-stack web hosting, especially Next.js and AI-generated front ends | $0 Hobby (100 GB transfer, personal/non-commercial only); $20/mo Pro | Pro includes 1 deploying seat, $20 usage credit, and 1 TB transfer; each extra deploying teammate adds $20/mo | Next.js apps, the cleanest beginner dashboard and preview flow, or commercial frontend teams |
| **Netlify** ([Pricing](https://www.netlify.com/pricing/) · [Credit rules](https://docs.netlify.com/manage/accounts-and-billing/billing/billing-for-credit-based-plans/how-credits-work/)) | Beginner-friendly web hosting with built-in forms and instant deploy previews | $0 Free forever (300 credits/mo); $9/mo Personal (1,000 credits); $20/mo Pro (3,000+ credits) | Single monthly credit pool covers deploys, bandwidth, and compute; Free pauses cleanly at 0, and **Pro has unlimited team seats** | Straightforward sites with built-in forms, or small teams wanting to avoid per-seat fees |
| **Supabase** ([Pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing) · [Spend cap](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/cost-control)) | Full relational database (PostgreSQL), user sign-up/login, and file storage | $0 Free (up to 2 active projects, 500 MB DB, 50,000 monthly active users); $25/mo Pro | Free projects pause after 7 idle days; Pro never pauses (8 GB disk, 250 GB data transfer), with extra running projects from $10/mo | Apps centered on real user accounts, relational data, and permissions where Cloudflare's light DB hits limits |
| **AWS** ([Free plan](https://aws.amazon.com/free/) · [Lightsail](https://aws.amazon.com/lightsail/pricing/) · [Amplify](https://aws.amazon.com/amplify/pricing/)) | Full-scale big cloud: fixed-price Linux servers, web hosting, heavy databases, and enterprise backends | Up to $200 credits on a 6-month Free plan + 30+ always-free quotas; Lightsail flat-rate servers from $5/mo ($3.50 IPv6-only) | Lightsail stays flat-rate like a phone plan; metered services like Amplify ($0.15/GB transfer after free tier) can spike if misconfigured | Off-the-shelf Linux software, heavy databases, long background jobs, or growing without ever migrating again |

![Side-by-side comparison of five mainstream providers: Cloudflare for $0-$5 lightweight all-in-one hosting, Vercel for Next.js and the cleanest frontend workflow, Netlify for credit-based web hosting with unlimited Pro seats, Supabase for full PostgreSQL databases and user logins, and AWS for standard Linux backends and ceiling-free scale](/img/blog/2026-11-28-ai-and-cloudflare-again/figure-3-providers-comparison.en.png)

In everyday terms, here is where each alternative genuinely beats Cloudflare:

- **For the cleanest beginner dashboard and Next.js apps, Vercel is still smoother.** If your AI tool generates a Next.js or standard React web app, Vercel feels noticeably less intimidating than Cloudflare—you rarely stumble into corporate security menus, and its preview workflow remains the gold standard. Just watch two pricing edges: under Vercel's [Terms of Service](https://vercel.com/legal/terms), the free Hobby tier is strictly for personal, non-commercial projects. The moment your project takes revenue, runs ads, or adds a collaborator who deploys, you move to Pro at $20/month—plus another $20/month for each extra deploying teammate.
- **For small teams that hate per-seat fees, Netlify is more flexible.** Netlify runs on a shared monthly credit pool that covers production deploys, bandwidth, and compute together. Its Free plan gives 300 credits every month with built-in form handling and pauses cleanly instead of surprise-charging when you run out; solo builders who outgrow Free can step up to the $9/month Personal tier. On the $20/month Pro tier, the standout perk is unlimited team members at no extra per-seat cost. The tradeoff is media-heavy traffic: at 20 credits per GB of bandwidth, popular image or download sites burn through credits much faster than on Cloudflare's zero-bandwidth-fee model.
- **Whenever real user accounts and a full database sit at the center, pick Supabase—and note that it pairs well with Cloudflare.** Cloudflare's lightweight database is great for simple trackers or settings, not for an app with customer accounts, orders, and multi-table queries. Supabase gives you a full open-source PostgreSQL database, a spreadsheet-like table viewer anyone can understand, and free built-in authentication for up to 50,000 monthly active users. The main free-tier catch is that projects automatically pause after 7 days of inactivity until you wake them manually. Upgrading to the $25/month Pro tier removes pausing and includes a toggleable [Spend Cap](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/cost-control) on usage overages. In practice, many solo builders do not choose between Cloudflare and Supabase at all—they host the website on Cloudflare or Vercel and let Supabase handle logins and core data.
- **When a project outgrows lightweight platforms or needs standard Linux software, AWS is the grown-up destination.** AWS is still the hardest console for a newcomer to walk into, packed with hundreds of services, permission rules, and metered pricing terms. Even so, AWS has softened the landing: new accounts now get a 6-month [Free plan](https://aws.amazon.com/free/) with up to $200 in credits and no charges unless you switch to paid. If you just want a standard Linux box that runs any open-source server app on a fixed monthly bill, [Amazon Lightsail](https://aws.amazon.com/lightsail/pricing/) starts at $5/month with a public IPv4 address—far simpler than raw EC2. AWS wins on headroom: if a weekend tool turns into a real business, you will not hit a platform ceiling and face a painful rewrite.

## So, should you pick Cloudflare?

Put the wins, the caveats, and the mainstream alternatives side by side, and the answer to our opening question is straightforward.

**Cloudflare is a genuinely strong starting point today if:**

- You are using AI to build a personal site, portfolio, lightweight tool, or early prototype and want to launch for $0 to $5 a month.
- Your users are outside mainland China (or it is just for you and a small circle), and you like having site hosting, a small backend, and file storage in one place.
- You worry about surprise bandwidth charges and want predictable pricing with a real spending cap.
- Your AI-generated project uses modern web tools like Vite and benefits from smoother out-of-the-box deployment.

**Skip it—or plan to move off it—if:**

- Your core audience is in mainland China and you do not have an enterprise budget and ICP filings.
- Your app needs a heavy traditional database, long-running background processing, or off-the-shelf Linux server software.
- Easy portability matters more to you than one-stop convenience, and you do not want backend data code tied to one vendor's interface.

In short, Cloudflare does not have to be your only tool: pick Vercel for the cleanest Next.js and frontend workflow, Netlify for small-team sites without per-seat fees, Supabase whenever real user accounts and a full database sit at the core (often paired with Cloudflare or Vercel on the front end), and AWS when you need standard Linux servers or room to grow without ceilings.

Back to the question mark in the title: **can AI make Cloudflare great again?**

For solo developers and everyday builders using AI, AI has already turned Cloudflare from an operations-team specialty into one of the most sensible places to put a small project online. Not because of flashy AI slogans, but because when AI makes creating software cheap, the platform that makes publishing it calm, inexpensive, and predictable wins the first step. It is not effortless yet—domains, debugging, and mainland China access still trip beginners up—but as a low-cost starting line in 2026, Cloudflare is far friendlier than its old reputation suggests.
