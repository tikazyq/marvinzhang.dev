This week I wanted to expose one of my internal dashboards so I could check it from outside the network. It took three tries. On the first pass I did it the old way, opening a port on nginx, and realized almost immediately that leaving it bare on the public internet was a non-starter. On the second pass I tried origin rules, which still left port exposure tangled up with authentication. On the third pass I switched to [Cloudflare Tunnel](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/networks/connectors/cloudflare-tunnel/) paired with [Cloudflare Access](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/access-controls/access-settings/session-management/), stretching the session length so I wouldn't have to sign back in every few hours. The real win was separating network reachability from access authorization: Tunnel gets the service out without exposing a public port, and Access decides who can enter and how long a session stays valid (longer sessions are smoother, though a compromised device stays exposed longer too). Once the dust settled, I turned that setup into my default pattern and put every domain, DNS record, Tunnel, and Access policy under declarative management with the [Cloudflare Terraform Provider](https://developers.cloudflare.com/terraform/).

Finishing that small chore left me with a much bigger question. For more than a decade, mention Cloudflare and most engineers think of CDN, WAF, and free DNS: a dependable networking and security base layer. Going back through its docs, recent launches, and earnings filings changed that picture. From frontend build tooling and model gateways to stateful agent runtimes and tools that block or charge AI crawlers on behalf of publishers, its bet on the AI wave goes much deeper than I expected.

{/* truncate */}

In [*The Last Mile of AI Is Infrastructure, Not Intelligence*](/blog/ai-last-mile-infrastructure), I argued that as models make writing code cheaper, the scarce layer becomes the infrastructure that gets that code to users safely and reliably. That post looked at the industry as a whole. This one zooms in on a single company: **can AI actually give Cloudflare a second growth curve?** Let me put my stance on the table up front. I am treating this as a hypothesis to test, not an earnings story already in the bag. Below, I walk through six lines of evidence: developer onboarding, inference and gateways, long-running agents, publisher monetization, public financials, and the flip side of competition, lock-in, correlated outages, and running in mainland China.

## Why developers and their agents start here

Whether a cloud platform captures the next wave of workloads is often decided long before any architecture review. Whoever lets a solo developer, a small team, or their coding agent stand up a first live URL with the least friction owns the front door.

Cloudflare has spent the past two years closing long-standing gaps in its console and developer experience (DX). On October 12, 2026, it [merged the main dashboard and Zero Trust navigation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-10-12-unified-dashboard-navigation/) so you no longer bounce between two separate admin panels. On the observability side, it went from the February 2026 [Observability UI refresh](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-02-06-observability-ui-refresh/) to a platform-wide [Cloudflare Traces public beta](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-tracing/) on October 2.

The path from local development to production is also converging on configuring the environment once. [Wrangler CLI](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/wrangler/) plus the [Cloudflare Vite plugin](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/vite-plugin/) let `vite dev` run server-side code directly on the open-source `workerd` runtime, locally simulating Workers, D1, KV, R2, and Durable Objects close to production behavior. [Workers Builds](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/ci-cd/builds/) added Git-triggered CI/CD on September 25, 2025; [Worker Previews](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-09-22-worker-previews/) shipped isolated environments per pull request on September 22, 2026; and the unified [`cf` CLI](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-cf-cli-launch/) landed on September 28. More tellingly, Cloudflare reshaped the entry path around how agents actually work: the docs surface a [Workers `llms.txt`](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/llms.txt) index right at the top, Cloudflare hosts official [MCP servers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/model-context-protocol/cloudflare/servers-for-cloudflare/) covering more than 2,500 API endpoints, and on June 19, 2026 it launched [60-minute accountless temporary Worker previews](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-06-19-temporary-accounts-for-agents/) so an agent can spin up a live artifact without even creating an account. As I noted in [*Stack Selection in the Agent Era*](/blog/stack-selection-in-the-agent-era), agents optimize for short feedback loops and machine-readable signals. Every piece here aims to drive the cost of trial and error to the floor.

The pricing ladder reinforces that front-door strategy. Cloudflare does not give everything away for free; it offers item-by-item free quotas paired with a low-friction entry tier, and each product comes with metering rules you need to read up front:

Lay that table out and the intent is plain. For $0 to $5 a month, an individual project or early team gets static hosting, edge compute, object storage, SQLite, stateful objects, free tunnels, and zero-trust access for up to 50 people. For indie builders and agent prototypes trying to get something live first and worry later, starting friction is hard to beat, while the upgrade steps are built in: once row scans, writes, or headcount grow, the meter shows up under those exact rules.

Another June 2026 deal pushed this onboarding strategy a step further. **VoidZero**, founded by Evan You and behind Vite, Vitest, Rolldown, Oxc, and Vite+, closed its acquisition on June 1 and announced it was [joining Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/voidzero-joins-cloudflare/) on June 4 ([Evan You's announcement](https://voidzero.dev/posts/voidzero-cloudflare)). Neither blog post mentioned a price, but Cloudflare's [Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1477333/000147733326000054/cloud-20260630.htm) filed August 6 (and reconfirmed in its [Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1477333/000147733326000061/cloud-20260930.htm)) put the actual numbers on the record: $164.2 million in acquisition consideration ($98.7 million in Class A stock plus $65.5 million in net cash), plus $106.4 million in separately accounted equity incentive arrangements.

It is easy to shorthand this as "Cloudflare bought the frontend ecosystem," so it helps to separate two things cleanly. First, acquiring a tooling company, employing its core team, and helping distribute its tools is not the same as seizing open-source ownership, changing licenses, or forcing deployments onto your own cloud. Both sides and the [Vite core team statement](https://vite.dev/blog/cloudflare-supports-vite) committed that Vite, Vitest, Rolldown, Oxc, and Vite+ remain MIT-licensed, vendor-neutral, and community-governed, with unrestricted deployment targets. Follow-through so far backs up part of that pledge: [Vite+ 1.0 shipped under the MIT license](https://voidzero.dev/posts/announcing-vite-plus-1-0) on September 28, and Cloudflare's pledged $1 million Vite Ecosystem Fund announced its first [$301,300](https://blog.cloudflare.com/launching-vite-ecosystem-fund/) in grants across 44 projects and maintainers on November 9.

Second, community wariness is still justified. In the June 4 [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48398055), some readers were relieved that core frontend tooling had a well-funded sponsor, while others worried the toolchain would gradually tilt toward Cloudflare hosting. On October 15, Cloudflare followed up with [Void](https://blog.cloudflare.com/deploy-with-void/), a native hosting service built for Vite. Put plainly, Cloudflare paid real money to bet on a developer-funnel hypothesis: faster frontend and full-stack tooling tightens the iteration loop for both humans and coding agents, and if official plugins plus Void make the path from local Vite to the edge effortless, some slice of that traffic stays on Cloudflare. Still, that remains a **strategic hypothesis**. Open-source adoption does not automatically equal a measured paid cloud conversion rate.

## Following an inference request: Workers AI vs. AI Gateway

Frontend tooling and a generous free tier do not add up to an AI growth story on their own. Once you actually run AI in production, the first problem is how inference requests leave your app, where they go, and what catches them when something breaks. Cloudflare splits that job across two very different products: **Workers AI** to run models itself, and **AI Gateway** to sit in front of third-party model providers.

Trace a single inference request and the split is clear. Traffic hits the nearest edge node, an Edge Worker picks it up, and from there it can take one of two paths:

1. Call [Workers AI](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/) directly to run open-weight models on Cloudflare's serverless edge GPUs, skipping GPU rental and autoscaling. Under [Workers AI pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/), every tier includes 10,000 free Neurons per day, and paid overages cost $0.011 per 1,000 Neurons (some newer large models require a payment method on file or prepaid AI Gateway credits).
2. Follow the more common production setup, where an app calls OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Bedrock, and open models side by side. Here the request passes through [AI Gateway](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/) as a single proxy that forwards to upstream providers and can fall back to Workers AI when needed.

![Two inference paths from a Cloudflare Edge Worker: Workers AI runs open-weight models on Cloudflare's serverless edge GPUs to sell compute, while AI Gateway proxies OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Bedrock, and Workers AI with caching, rate limiting, fallbacks, free basic DLP, Unified Billing, and Auto Router to own the traffic control plane](/img/blog/2026-11-28-ai-and-cloudflare-again/figure-1-inference-paths.en.png)

The commercial difference between those two layers matters a lot. Workers AI sells edge compute, putting it head-to-head with dedicated model hosts and hyperscaler GPUs on price, throughput, and model coverage. That is a capital-heavy grind. AI Gateway stakes out the traffic entry point and control plane: caching repeat prompts, rate-limiting runaway agent loops before they burn through a budget, handling multi-provider retries and failover, and running basic data loss prevention (DLP) scans inline. Per [AI Gateway pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/reference/pricing/), core features including analytics, caching, rate limiting, and basic DLP are free across all tiers (for accounts created on or after September 24, 2026, gateway logs follow Workers Logs billing and retention rules).

Several 2026 updates pulled those two layers tighter together. On August 7, Cloudflare [unified bindings and credit billing](https://blog.cloudflare.com/workers-ai-gateway-unification/) across Workers AI and AI Gateway. With [Unified Billing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/unified-billing/), teams can pay multiple upstream providers out of a single prepaid Cloudflare balance (with a 5% credit purchase fee on top of upstream list prices). On September 30, [Auto Router entered public beta](https://blog.cloudflare.com/auto-router/), evaluating candidate models by task type and difficulty and routing automatically across the quality-cost tradeoff. Drop one layer down to data, and [R2's zero egress fees](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/) paired with [Basin](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-10-01-basin-ga/) (an Iceberg catalog and data pipeline that hit GA on October 1) open a practical multi-cloud wedge: AI teams can keep training corpora and intermediate artifacts in R2 and rent GPUs on whichever cloud is cheapest without paying S3 egress fees every time.

In short, Workers AI wants revenue from running inference on Cloudflare's own GPUs. AI Gateway bets that even when your priciest frontier-model spend goes elsewhere, the traffic and billing still pass through Cloudflare. One enters the compute business; the other adapts Cloudflare's classic CDN and WAF playbook to LLM API traffic.

## A long-running agent: state, MCP, and shipped sandboxes

A single Q&A turn is just a stateless HTTP request that any cloud function can serve. What breaks older architectures is a **long-running agent**: something that holds context across hours or days, wakes up on a schedule, waits for human approval, talks to several external tools, and picks up where it left off after an error. I wrote about running multi-session agents in [*Managing Managers: When an AI Starts Running AI Managers*](/blog/managing-managers-a-chief-pm-agent-and-the-coordination-tax). Once work stretches across turns and tools, the real engineering drain is usually persisting state and isolating where code runs.

Take an agent that continuously triages tickets, searches docs, and occasionally runs verification scripts. Cloudflare has assembled a stateful stack built around three pieces:

- **A stateful execution home**: The [Cloudflare Agents SDK](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/) sits directly on top of [Durable Objects](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/). Each agent instance maps to a globally unique object with its own [embedded SQLite storage](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/best-practices/access-durable-objects-storage/). It can hibernate when idle to stop duration billing, then wake immediately on an incoming message or alarm. Longer multi-step jobs with retries can hand off to [Workflows](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-04-07-workflows-ga/), which is generally available. Along the same track, April 2026's [Project Think](https://blog.cloudflare.com/project-think/) and the November 20 release of [Pi 1.6](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-11-20-pi-harness-pi-1-6/) flesh out persistent execution loops that pause for approval and hibernate.
- **Remote MCP evolution and enterprise controls**: On August 6, 2026, Cloudflare shifted away from its earlier pattern toward stateless [MCP SDK v2](https://blog.cloudflare.com/mcp-v2/) (`createMcpHandler`) and [deprecated the older `McpAgent`](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/model-context-protocol/guides/migrate-to-mcp-sdk-v2/). On September 24, [MCP Server Portals reached GA](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-09-24-mcp-portals-ga/), placing enterprise-approved remote MCP servers behind Cloudflare Access for unified auth, logging, and DLP.
- **Two shipped execution sandboxes**: When an agent has to juggle dozens or hundreds of tools, or run real code, Cloudflare offers a light path and a heavy path. The light path is [Code Mode](https://blog.cloudflare.com/code-mode), which turns MCP tools into typed TypeScript interfaces so the model writes a short script and executes multi-step calls in one go inside a lightweight isolate via [Dynamic Workers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-03-24-dynamic-workers-open-beta/) (open beta since March 24, 2026), passing only the final result back into model context. The heavy path is [Containers and Sandbox](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-04-13-containers-sandbox-ga/), GA since April 13, 2026 (paid, metered by active vCPU, memory, disk, and egress; see [Containers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/platform/pricing/)). On September 30, Cloudflare followed with [Sandbox SDK 1.0](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-09-30-sandbox-sdk-1-0/), letting an agent's Durable Object mount an isolated [Linux container microVM](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/tools/sandbox/) to install packages, compile code, and persist files across turns, separated from the agent's own storage and credentials.

Put these together and Cloudflare has genuine differentiation in agent infrastructure: Durable Objects hold state, while V8 isolates and Linux containers execute code, re-packaging its existing edge primitives into an agent runtime. The catch is that the smoother this stack feels, the steeper the lock-in and shared-dependency bill becomes. We will tally that in the last section.

## The publisher side: from blocking crawlers to charging per crawl

Cloudflare holds one card that pure compute clouds cannot easily copy: a large share of the public web already sits behind its network. LLM training and AI search upended publisher traffic economics, with bots scraping full articles while sending fewer clicks back to the origin. Cloudflare built three tiers of tooling around that friction.

The first tier is **declaring intent**. Through managed [`robots.txt` and Content Signals policies](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/additional-configurations/managed-robots-txt/), site owners can specify allow or disallow rules by use case such as Search, Agents, and Training. As Cloudflare's own documentation puts it plainly, `robots.txt` relies on voluntary compliance and cannot technically stop a crawler that ignores it.

The second tier is **edge visibility and blocking**. [AI Crawl Control](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-crawl-control/) ([launched August 27, 2025](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-08-27-ai-crawl-control-launch/)) shows site owners which AI crawlers are hitting their pages and how frequently, then lets them allow or block traffic at the edge. One widely repeated oversimplification deserves a correction here. On July 1, 2025, Cloudflare made headlines by announcing a move to a [permission-based access model](https://www.cloudflare.com/press/press-releases/2025/cloudflare-just-changed-how-ai-crawlers-scrape-the-internet-at-large/). According to the [November 2, 2026 official docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/additional-configurations/block-ai-bots/), however, standard newly onboarded domains still default to `Allow` for Search, Agents, and Training; only sites that choose the ad-monetized publisher onboarding path get `Disallow` on Training by default.

The third tier is the boldest and also the easiest to overhype: **direct monetization**. [Pay Per Crawl](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-crawl-control/features/pay-per-crawl/what-is-pay-per-crawl/) entered [private beta on July 1, 2025](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-07-01-pay-per-crawl/), dusting off the HTTP 402 (Payment Required) status code. A crawler sending payment intent gets a 200 and the content; without it, the site returns a 402 and a price quote, with Cloudflare acting as merchant of record to settle transactions. Dynamic pricing followed in June 2026, and [Pay Per Use entered open beta](https://blog.cloudflare.com/pay-per-use/) on September 30 to handle downstream usage-based reporting and settlement.

When judging how much this adds to Cloudflare's growth curve, we have to stay strictly inside what is verified. Seventeen months after launch, **Pay Per Crawl is still in private beta**, and actual settlement volume and publisher payouts have never been disclosed. On the [Q3 2026 earnings call transcript](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/net/transcripts/726816-q3-2026/), management noted recording its first "Act 4" revenue without naming the specific product or dollar amount, and separately mentioned exploring ways with OpenAI and others to pay for fresher data. Those two points should not be conflated, and neither establishes measurable revenue scale yet. Major media groups have legal teams to negotiate blanket licensing deals directly with model labs, while small and mid-sized sites face an awkward tradeoff: put up a tollbooth and risk disappearing from AI retrieval altogether. Until real transaction volume and public financial contribution appear, publisher monetization is an interesting option, nowhere near a proven business model.

Developer and AI operational signals are expanding alongside the top line. Active developers on the platform climbed from [over 4.5 million](https://www.cloudflare.net/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/CORRECTED-TRANSCRIPT-Cloudflare-Inc-NET-US-Q4-2025-Earnings-Call-10-February-2026-5-00-PM-ET.pdf) at the end of 2025 to [over 10 million](https://www.cloudflare.com/press/press-releases/2026/more-than-10-million-developers-are-building-on-cloudflare/) announced on October 20, 2026. In the June 2026 [Investor Day transcript](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/net/transcripts/649448-investor-day-2026/), management estimated that roughly 80% of leading AI companies use at least one Cloudflare service (keep in mind that count includes traditional CDN and WAF). Earnings calls also highlighted several large usage-based Pool of Funds deals: a [2-year, $85 million](https://www.cloudflare.net/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/CORRECTED-TRANSCRIPT-Cloudflare-Inc-NET-US-Q4-2025-Earnings-Call-10-February-2026-5-00-PM-ET.pdf) full-platform deal with an AI company in Q4 2025; two 1-year developer platform Pool of Funds deals on the Q2 2026 call for [$7.5 million (used for generative AI storage and R2) and $6.0 million (used for agent containers)](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/net/transcripts/662709-q2-2026/); and a [1-year, $11 million](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/net/transcripts/726816-q3-2026/) Workers developer platform deal called out on the Q3 2026 call. Keep one accounting line sharp: those figures are contract commitments, not recognized period revenue.

Now put two hard constraints right next to those numbers, so we don't fill in the blanks with wishful thinking.

The first constraint is a **disclosure gap**. In its SEC filings ([Q3 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1477333/000147733326000061/cloud-20260930.htm)), Cloudflare reports consolidated revenue under a single operating segment. It does not break out AI revenue or AI's share of total revenue, nor does it report standalone revenue for the Workers developer platform, Workers AI, AI Gateway, R2, or Act 4 / Pay Per Crawl. From public disclosures alone, nobody outside the company knows how much of Q3's 39% growth came from AI workloads versus core network security, SASE, and stronger enterprise sales execution. One common trap to avoid: the 20.18 million monthly AI Gateway requests and 51.83 billion Workers AI tokens cited in Cloudflare's [internal AI engineering stack post](https://blog.cloudflare.com/internal-ai-engineering-stack/) are Cloudflare's own internal usage, not external customer volume.

The second constraint is **mild gross margin and cash flow pressure**. In Q3 2026, Non-GAAP operating margin expanded to 17.5%, yet GAAP gross margin slipped from 74.0% a year earlier to 71.2%, and free cash flow margin fell from 13% to 10% ([Q3 2026 Release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1477333/000147733326000060/q326exhibit991.htm)). Compute-, bandwidth-, and storage-heavy infrastructure simply has a different cost structure than pure software security services. Growth is up; whether Cloudflare can defend its margins as that mix shifts will take several more quarters to answer.

## The other side: competitors, lock-in, correlated outages, and China deployment

Every stack choice comes with a bill. Putting more of your system on one platform buys the convenience I started with, where one Terraform repo runs everything, and charges you in four concrete ways.

**First, external competition.** In the enterprise core, AWS still has deep VPC gravity through [Bedrock PrivateLink](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/vpc-interface-endpoints.html) and flagship GPU clusters such as [EC2 P6](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/instance-types/p6/). Cloudflare's sharpest wedge there is zero-egress R2 positioned directly against [S3 egress pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/). In frontend and full-stack developer onboarding, Vercel still commands React and Next.js mindshare and offers its own [AI SDK / AI Gateway](https://vercel.com/kb/guide/ai-gateway-and-ai-sdk) and [Sandbox](https://vercel.com/kb/guide/running-ai-generated-code-sandbox); Cloudflare's VoidZero acquisition takes direct aim at that funnel. On the edge-network turf Cloudflare grew up on, Fastly shipped semantic caching with [AI Accelerator](https://www.fastly.com/documentation/guides/platform/about-the-ai-accelerator-page/), and Akamai launched distributed edge orchestration with [AI Grid](https://www.ir.akamai.com/news-releases/news-release-details/akamai-launches-ai-grid-intelligent-orchestration-distributed). Cloudflare's advantage is wiring networking, security, and stateful edge compute onto a single network, not monopolizing any single piece.

**Second, architectural lock-in.** Even though `workerd` is open source and R2 speaks the S3 API, Workers only implements a [subset of Node.js APIs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/nodejs/), and [Python Workers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/languages/python/) run on Pyodide and WebAssembly. Once an agent app wires identity, WebSockets, SQLite state, and vector search deep into the Agents SDK, Durable Objects, D1, and Vectorize, moving off means rewriting your state layer and bindings almost from scratch.

**Third, single-vendor blast radius.** Consolidating DNS, Tunnel, Access, edge compute, and state onto one vendor collapses independent failure domains into a single shared one. Public outage records from late 2025 through 2026 show that coupling clearly. On December 5, 2025, a global WAF test config change hit a legacy proxy bug and caused [roughly 28% of the HTTP traffic Cloudflare was serving to return 500s](https://blog.cloudflare.com/5-december-2025-outage/) for 25 minutes. In 2026, Durable Objects repeatedly transmitted faults downstream: a June 22 eastern North America fiber cut hit CDN, analytics, and [Durable Objects for 7 hours 47 minutes](https://www.cloudflarestatus.com/incidents/v3yl7jqmqj51); July 16 Eurasia [Durable Objects errors](https://isdown.app/status/cloudflare/incidents/623773-durable-objects-increased-errors) cascaded into Workflows, Containers, and D1 for roughly 1.7 hours; and November 17 brought [2 hours 21 minutes of Durable Objects timeouts](https://www.cloudflarestatus.com/incidents/ylj5po752g6h) affecting Stream and Containers. Even after Cloudflare completed its [Fail Small fault-isolation overhaul](https://blog.cloudflare.com/code-orange-fail-small-complete/) in May 2026, [Cloudflare One Gateway traffic using identity policies was impacted for 3 hours 12 minutes on October 20, hitting the dashboard as well](https://www.cloudflarestatus.com/incidents/0eqtn0khkz87), and on November 26 [the dashboard and API returned errors for 2 hours 14 minutes](https://www.cloudflarestatus.com/incidents/xiqj67vl5pqm). Putting the whole stack on one provider trims day-to-day operational overhead, at the price of shared dependencies shaking together.

**Fourth, the reality of serving mainland China.** Start with Cloudflare's standard global network. A 2026 [research paper](https://secretlab.page/publications/block-a-mole-arxiv-2026.pdf) using OONI measurement data found that 984 out of 1,100 tested `workers.dev` domains were unreachable directly from mainland China. Even with custom domains, cross-border congestion causing high latency and packet loss is a limitation Cloudflare itself acknowledges in its [China Express post](https://blog.cloudflare.com/china-express/). Turn to the compliant JD Cloud-operated [Cloudflare China Network](https://developers.cloudflare.com/china-network/) and the bar rises fast: it is sold only to Enterprise customers as a separate subscription, requires an MIIT ICP filing or license for each primary domain plus JD Cloud content review, and automatically turns on IPv6 for onboarded domains. More importantly, check the [China Network available products list](https://developers.cloudflare.com/china-network/reference/available-products/): it includes Workers and KV, yet explicitly **prohibits creating R2 buckets or binding R2 custom domains inside mainland China** (cross-border R2 access requires separate Global Acceleration). Core AI-era pieces like Workers AI, AI Gateway, Durable Objects, and the Agents SDK are not on the China-available list at all.

So when should you skip Cloudflare? The rules are straightforward. If your core users are in mainland China and you lack enterprise budget and ICP filings, domestic cloud providers will save you far more trouble. If your workload centers on heavy relational databases, long-running compute, or flagship GPU training clusters, AWS and self-managed clusters still fit better. And if compliance or disaster-recovery policies require strict separation of failure domains, do not tie ingress, zero trust, and compute to a single vendor.

Back to the question mark in the title: **can AI make Cloudflare great again?**

My answer is that the question mark still has to stay. AI has clearly handed Cloudflare a much stronger deck than plain CDN: low-friction onboarding for developers and coding agents, AI Gateway standing in front of multi-model traffic, a stateful edge runtime suited to long-running agents, and perimeter leverage over public web crawlers. Put that alongside 39% Q3 revenue growth and a 10-million-plus developer base, and it is easy to see why builders like me reach for it more often when starting something new.

Still, there are three falsifiable gates between "developers like starting here" and "a financial-statement-verified second growth curve": whether Cloudflare can break out AI or developer platform revenue separately over the coming quarters while showing high growth does not require steady gross margin erosion; how much of the 10-million-plus developer base and the VoidZero frontend ecosystem converts into recurring, expanding enterprise spend; and whether Pay Per Crawl and Pay Per Use can graduate from beta into meaningful settlement volume. Until those three gates are cleared, Cloudflare has proven itself one of the smoothest default starting points of the AI era, but calling it an AI-reinvented Cloudflare is still premature.
